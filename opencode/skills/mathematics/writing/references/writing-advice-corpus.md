# The Mathematical Writing Advice Corpus

Practitioner advice pages, where the specific rules live. Parent:
[[mathematics/writing/writing|mathematical writing]]; the canonical texts (Halmos, Serre,
Knuth, Mermin, Higham, Krantz, Su) are at the top of
[exposition-style.md](exposition-style.md).

Working mathematicians maintain advice pages, and they are the highest-value source for
this system precisely because they are **specific and out of distribution**: rules about
when to number an equation, how to name a lemma, where the reader's attention goes in a
proof, which phrase is dead. A model reproduces general style advice from priors. It does
not reproduce these.

## Individual advice pages

- **Terence Tao, "On writing"** — terrytao.wordpress.com/advice-on-writing-papers/,
  including "Write professionally". A structured set of pages on drafting, submission,
  and the mechanics of a paper, with links out to the pages below.
- **Keith Conrad, "Advice on Mathematical Writing"** —
  kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/proofs/writingtips.pdf, inside a collection of over 250
  expository blurbs at kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/. Paired good and bad examples,
  which is the form that transfers best.
- **Igor Pak, "How to Write a Clear Math Paper: Some 21st Century Tips"** — *Journal of
  Humanistic Mathematics* 8(1), 2018. Deliberately opinionated and current: arXiv-era
  conventions, structure, and what clarity costs. His blog (igorpak.wordpress.com) carries
  more, e.g. "How to start a paper?".
- **Ravi Vakil, "For potential Ph.D. students"** — math.stanford.edu/~vakil/, on how to
  learn and present a theory: toy cases, explicit examples, what to calculate before
  writing anything down.
- **Dan Margalit, "Mathematics Students Resource"** — margalit.droppages.net/tsr/, a
  professional-development resource for beginning researchers in geometry and topology.
- **Ashley Reiter, "Writing a Research Paper in Mathematics"** —
  web.mit.edu/jrickert/www/mathadvice.html. Structure of a first paper, from the MIT
  materials.
- **John Tsitsiklis, "A Few Tips on Writing Papers with Mathematical Content"** —
  mit.edu/~jnt/Papers/R-20-write-v5.pdf.
- **Nicholas Higham's blog** — nhigham.com, which carries the specific tips that did not
  fit the Handbook.

## Anthologies

- **"Advice to a Young Mathematician"**, in Gowers (ed.), *The Princeton Companion to
  Mathematics*, VIII.6 — five independent essays by Atiyah, Bollobás, Connes, McDuff and
  Sarnak. Writing sits inside advice about the practice as a whole, which is the right
  framing for it.

## Aggregators, for the dozens more

These exist to collect exactly this material, and are the efficient way to extend the
list rather than rediscovering pages one at a time:

- **MAA Mathematical Communication** — mathcomm.org, originating in MIT's
  communication-intensive mathematics courses; its `/writing/` section covers written
  genres and general principles.
- **AMS Blog on Math Blogs**, "A roundup of advice for writing about mathematics".
- **University of Michigan, "Advice from Mathematicians around the Web"**, on their
  graduate resources site.

## Finding more

- `<mathematician name> advice writing mathematics` and `<name> expository` — most pages
  are personal-site subpages with no consistent title.
- `mathematical writing tips site:.edu` — department and personal pages dominate.
- `"advice for" "graduate students" mathematics writing` — the genre's usual framing.
- Follow the outbound links from Tao's writing page and from the aggregators above; this
  literature is heavily cross-referenced, and two hops reach most of it.

## How to use this here

Take the specific rule, not the general sentiment. These pages are worth reading because
they say things like *put the hypothesis in the statement rather than the surrounding
prose*, *do not use a symbol before defining it even by one line*, *a lemma nobody uses
twice is a step and should be inlined* — rules with a sharp boundary that can be checked
against a draft. General exhortations to be clear are already in the priors and add
nothing.
